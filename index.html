Abstract

This report presents the design, simulation (NI Multisim), and laboratory breadboard implementation of a complete DC power supply integrated with a low-power BJT common-emitter amplifier. The circuit is structured as four cascaded stages: a full-wave bridge rectifier converting 9 Vrms / 60 Hz AC into pulsating DC at a measured peak of 12.6 V; a 100 µF capacitive filter reducing peak-to-peak ripple to 880 mV; a 6.2 V Zener diode regulator with a 470 Ω series resistor providing stable regulated output; and a 2N3904 NPN BJT common-emitter amplifier biased at VCE = 2.18 V yielding a measured gain of |Aᵥ| ≈ 3.75 at 105 kHz. Simulation results were benchmarked against experimental measurements, with key error sources including real diode forward drops, capacitor ESR, and high-frequency gain roll-off. All design objectives were successfully achieved.

S4
BJT Common-Emitter Amplifier
2N3904 NPN · VCC from Stage 3 · Empirically redesigned in lab
✓ Amplifying

The initial Multisim design (R1=47kΩ, R2=15kΩ, RC=3.3kΩ, RE=820Ω) failed in simulation due to a Q-point mismatch caused by the limited VCC = 6.2 V supply and the substitute Zener's unknown voltage shifting the bias point. The group diagnosed the problem from oscilloscope output smaller than input, then iteratively adjusted biasing resistors on the breadboard until clean amplification with 180° phase inversion was achieved.

Gain pattern explained: As the potentiometer resistance decreases, the load draws more current and pulls down the input signal at the base, reducing Vin_pp from 2.88 V → 1.52 V. The output amplitude remains stable (~10.6–10.8 V), so the computed ratio increases. This is not a transistor gain increase but rather input-side loading; the underlying transistor amplification remains consistent, confirming stable active-region operation. The moderate absolute gain values are partly attributed to operating at 105 kHz, near the bandwidth limit of the 2N3904.

Conclusions & Key Lessons

01
Always verify and record the actual Zener part number and measured breakdown voltage before proceeding to dependent stages. A substituted component of unknown voltage directly caused the Stage 4 Q-point failure.
02
BJT biasing is highly sensitive to VCC. Any change in supply voltage requires recalculation of bias resistors. Simulation success with one voltage does not guarantee lab success with a different supply.
03
Test amplifiers at lower frequencies (1–10 kHz) to approach near-theoretical BJT gain. At 105 kHz, internal parasitic capacitances (Cbe, Cbc) of the 2N3904 begin to attenuate gain significantly.
Future improvements: Use a 1000 µF filter capacitor to achieve sub-100 mV ripple; source a confirmed 6.2 V Zener (BZX55C6V2); increase VCC to 9–12 V to provide greater Q-point stability margin; and characterize the amplifier across a frequency sweep (Bode plot) to identify the −3 dB bandwidth of the 2N3904 stage.
